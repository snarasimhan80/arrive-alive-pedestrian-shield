How the arithmetic works. Each placement's effect is its share of this hour's modelled crash load multiplied by an assumed suppression rate for that asset against that cause, with diminishing returns on the second and third unit at the same location. Every one of those rates is a stated assumption, listed in the audit panel below, and every one of them is replaceable with a measured value from the pilot's own data. Nothing here is a black box.

Module three
Same engine, second problem

Pedestrian Shield — why blocked footpaths are a police number

Footpath encroachment is usually handed to the municipal corporation as a nuisance. That framing is why it never gets fixed. Pedestrians are 42% of this city's road victims — 121 of 287 deaths in the year last reported. A blocked footpath is the mechanism that puts them in the carriageway. Measured that way it stops being GHMC's housekeeping and becomes the largest single category in your own fatality register.

Detection pipelineYOLOv8n-seg · human review
  1. Map the footpath once per camera as a fixed polygon. A one-time five-minute task per pole.
  2. Detect and measure overlap — the model flags visible vehicle overlap with the marked footpath. It also labels people; a cross-section provides a provisional width-lost estimate. An officer checks each flag.
  3. Estimate displacement — width lost and pedestrian flow produce a planning estimate. Short visual tracks can also flag possible footpath-to-road movements for review; neither proves why a person entered the road.
  4. Route to whoever can clear it — a GHMC work order, not a challan, is the proposed pilot workflow. Automatic work-order routing is not part of this demo.
Footpath cross-sectionExposure mechanism

The proposed metric estimates how many pedestrians could be affected by lost walking width, then weights that estimate by traffic speed. It is a relative exposure index, not a measured crash probability or a count of people forced into traffic.

Module four
Closes the loop between two and three

Every patrol shift is a survey

The bikes the allocator sends out are also the cheapest survey fleet the city will ever own. A phone on the handlebar mount, capturing a frame every few metres, turns each patrol route into a continuous record of footpath, kerb and road-surface condition — with no server, no cloud account and no data leaving the device until an officer chooses to send it.

What the patrol phone doesOn-device · no server
  1. Patrol mode. Capture a frame every few metres while the bike is moving. Night frames are brightness-corrected on the phone automatically, so the fatigue window is covered as well as daylight.
  2. Local pre-filter first. The same YOLOv8n detector from module three screens every frame on the phone in about a tenth of a second. Only frames that show an obstruction, a pothole or a stationary vehicle go any further — so a full night shift costs a few dozen model calls, not thousands.
  3. Verify and describe. Flagged frames get a second, richer read: what the obstruction is, how much walking width it takes, whether it is a safety hazard or a nuisance.
  4. Deduplicate. Anything already recorded a few metres away in the last day is merged, so a stall that is there every morning is one record with a history, not thirty.
  5. Draft, never send. The phone drafts the report in English or Telugu. The officer reads it and sends it. Nothing goes out automatically.

Every finding leaves with an owner attached. A report that lands with the right zonal officer, at the right chainage, with a photograph and a timestamp, gets acted on. A report that lands in a general inbox does not. The routing table is the difference between a survey and a backlog, and it is maintained by the pilot's nodal officer, not by us.

Why this matters to the pilot. It answers the question you will ask about module three — "where does the footage come from?" — without a single new camera: the bikes module two already deploys are the sensors. It keeps every image on a department phone until an officer decides otherwise, so there is no data-protection question to clear before starting. And it means the pilot generates its own training data: every frame an officer confirms or rejects improves the local model, so the system is measurably better at week ten than at week one — which is the self-improving loop this whole proposal rests on.

Before you ask

What is real here, and what is not

A demo that overstates itself is worth less than no demo. This is the honest split, and it is on the page rather than in the footnotes because you will ask and the answer should not need to be extracted.

Real

  • Every headline figure — 2.80 lakh vehicles, 5–6 crashes a day, the 33/25/15/14 causation split, 42% pedestrian share, 121 pedestrian deaths, 65 identified locations, 19 ORR exits, the 30 sponsored patrol bikes. All published; all sourced at the foot of this page.
  • The Module 3 detector runs real local-model inference on uploaded video or photos. Its flags and short tracks need human review; the supplied sample footage is synthetic.
  • The arithmetic — the hourly model, the exposure index and the allocator all run live in this page. Move the clock and watch them recompute. Nothing is a pre-rendered picture.
  • The calibration — the ORR model is pinned so its 24-hour total reproduces your own published 5.5 crashes a day. It cannot drift away from your number.

Assumed, and replaceable within the pilot

  • The hourly shape of each cause. Fatigue peaking before dawn and tyre bursts peaking in afternoon heat are well-established patterns, but the Hyderabad-specific curves need your timestamped crash records. That is the single most valuable dataset you hold.
  • Per-exit traffic and heavy-vehicle share — inferred from what each exit connects to. Your toll gantry counts replace this immediately and for free.
  • Suppression rates per asset — how much a bike or a marshal actually reduces crashes. Assumed conservatively; measurable directly from a controlled pilot.
  • The ten pedestrian stand-ins — illustrative until replaced by uploaded analyses. Real path widths, peak-hour flow and gate distances still require site measurements.
  • The street photograph is not Hyderabad CCTV. Production accuracy on autos, hand-carts and two-wheelers requires fine-tuning on local footage — IIIT-Hyderabad's India Driving Dataset was filmed on these roads and is the obvious starting corpus.
The pilot
Ten weeks · ₹10 lakh · paid on acceptance

A ten-week paid pilot, with nothing paid until it has been measured

Ten weeks: two to onboard the corridor's crash records and camera feeds, six of shadow operation, two to evaluate against the baseline. The fee is ₹10 lakh, payable only at week ten and only if the agreed result is met. It can come through a corporate sponsor — the route that already delivered 30 patrol bikes to this commissionerate — or through the state's startup procurement route, which at this size needs no tender and no earnest-money deposit. Either way there is no capital sanction and no budget line for the department to defend before the result exists.

One corridor and one nodal officer for ten weeks

The ORR between two named exits, plus five to ten city junctions from your existing 65. Access to timestamped crash records for the corridor and to the camera feeds already on those poles, and four patrol bikes carrying a phone mount. No new hardware, no new poles, no live-system integration in the pilot phase.

A baseline agreed in writing, and shadow mode against it

The system predicts and recommends; it commands nothing and issues no challan. The acceptance test is fixed before week one: whether the placements it recommended would have sat where the corridor's crashes actually happened, and how much of the pedestrian-exposure ranking your own officers confirm on the ground. Meet it and the ₹10 lakh is paid. Miss it and nothing is.

An introduction, if it goes well

To the TG-TMARS working group under the Chief Secretary, and to T-AIM. A corridor result that holds up is the only credential worth carrying into that room, and it is the one thing we cannot manufacture ourselves.

What we are not asking for. No data-sharing agreement covering personal information, no access to any live enforcement system, no advance, and no payment before a measured result. If the ten weeks show nothing, the pilot ends, the department has spent nothing, and it carries no obligation to continue.
